# Casual / personal-growth learning context

## Decision

Cogni's sixth learning context is **Casual / personal growth**, with the shorter in-product identity **Everyday learner** and internal slug `casual`.

It is a **motivation and context segment, not an ability tier, age band, career stage or low-commitment version of Cogni**. A casual learner may be highly capable and highly motivated; the defining characteristic is that they want curiosity-led, practical learning outside a specific university or professional role.

## Research basis

UNESCO treats adult learning as lifelong and spanning formal, non-formal and informal settings. Its definition of informal learning includes self-directed learning in daily life, families, communities and online spaces. That supports a context built around real-life claims and choices rather than an institutional curriculum.

OECD research shows that adult learners have varied motivations, including personal goals and practical life skills. Its 2026 learner profiles explicitly include **Self-Directed Enthusiasts** and **Mixed-Motivation Lifeskills Learners**, demonstrating that career advancement is not the only meaningful adult-learning motivation.

OECD research also identifies lack of time as the most common participation barrier and finds that perceived usefulness and relevance are associated with participation. The product implication is short, flexible sessions with immediately recognisable value.

Research on learning design supports active retrieval, deep questions, spaced practice, autonomy and useful informational feedback. Cogni therefore keeps the same adaptive engine, skill measurement and mixed interaction formats as every other audience rather than turning casual learning into passive reading or lightweight trivia.

## Target learner

The context is designed for someone who:

- wants to think more clearly for personal rather than role-specific reasons;
- learns from curiosity, self-development or a current real-life question;
- wants better judgement about online information, AI answers, products, health claims, money, travel, habits, relationships or community issues;
- values short sessions that can fit around work, family and other responsibilities;
- may not identify as a student, graduate, professional, manager or executive for the purpose of learning;
- still deserves challenging, adaptive content and honest evidence about progress.

## Explicit exclusions

Casual does **not** mean:

- beginner, less intelligent or less capable;
- unserious, disengaged or unwilling to practise;
- a child mode;
- entertainment-only trivia;
- simplified content with weak reasoning standards;
- medical, legal, financial or safety advice.

Difficulty remains adaptive around a complexity anchor of **50**, similar to graduate / early-career content, and can rise or fall with the learner's measured performance.

## Product promise

> Build sharper judgement for everyday choices, online information and AI.

The daily session label is **Everyday practice** and the completion outcome is **Everyday judgement strengthened**.

## Personalisation

Casual learners can optionally select an **Interest area**:

- Everyday decisions
- News, media & online information
- Technology & AI
- Money, purchases & reviews
- Health & wellbeing information
- Relationships & communication
- Travel & planning
- Community & civic issues
- Personal growth & habits

They can optionally select a goal:

- Make better everyday decisions
- Understand news and online information
- Use AI more wisely
- Spot misleading claims
- Ask better questions
- Think more clearly
- Build a learning habit

Professional industry, role, responsibility and organisation fields are deliberately hidden because they are not necessary to define this context.

## Content taxonomy

The first full content bank covers eight recurring everyday scenario families:

1. health and wellbeing claims;
2. purchases, reviews and sponsorship;
3. AI-assisted travel and planning;
4. news, charts and short data windows;
5. community choices and vivid-event bias;
6. apps, privacy and data permissions;
7. personal trackers, habits and causal claims;
8. money, urgency, family trust and AI-generated claims.

Every scenario is crossed with all 15 Cogni judgement skills. High-consequence health, money, rights, privacy and safety choices consistently reinforce authoritative verification and human responsibility.

## Parity standard

Casual has been implemented to the same product depth as the existing audiences:

- 126 published challenges;
- 5 audience-specific starting-check questions;
- all 15 skills covered;
- 16 published daily lessons;
- 55 single-choice, 18 multi-select, 19 ranking, 17 classification and 17 triage questions;
- adaptive difficulty range 46–54 with a mean around 51;
- web onboarding, settings, dashboard and analytics support;
- native Android onboarding, Home and Profile support;
- optional interest and goal personalisation;
- server-side answer keys, scoring, XP, streaks, session resumption and feedback unchanged.
